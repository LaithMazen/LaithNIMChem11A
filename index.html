4. Quantitative chemistry

Worked example: Ti-6Al-4V (1000 g)

Composition by mass: 6% Al, 4% V, 90% Ti (the alloy used in engine parts and fasteners).

Moles = mass ÷ Ar
Ti: 900 ÷ 47.87 = 18.80 mol
Al: 60 ÷ 26.98 = 2.22 mol
V: 40 ÷ 50.94 = 0.785 mol
Total = 21.81 mol, so atom % Ti = 18.80 ÷ 21.81 = 86.2%, Al = 10.2%, V = 3.6%.

Although Al is only 6% by mass, it is about 10% of the atoms, because its atoms are lighter.

5. Why lightweight matters

For level flight, lift must equal weight. A heavier aircraft needs more lift, so a larger angle of attack and more drag, so more thrust, so more fuel burned. Extra fuel is itself extra weight, which is why savings snowball. Fuel burned releases CO₂: about 3.16 kg of CO₂ per 1 kg of jet fuel (ICAO factor).

Composites make up roughly 50% of the Boeing 787 and about 53% of the Airbus A350 by weight, versus around 25% on the A380. That is a key reason the newer types burn less fuel per passenger.

6. Sustainability evaluation

UAE connection

The UAE is aiming for net zero by 2050, and Emirates and Etihad are both investing in fuel-efficient fleets and sustainable aviation fuel. Etihad's Greenliner programme uses commercial flights to test efficiency ideas. The UAE also makes aluminium at scale: Emirates Global Aluminium (EGA) launched CelestiAL, aluminium smelted with solar power, and recycled aluminium needs only around 5% of the energy of primary production.

7. Final reflection

How can developments in materials chemistry contribute to more sustainable aviation in the UAE?

Every kilogram removed from an aircraft saves fuel on every flight for decades, so lighter materials cut emissions directly. Chemistry can go further: (1) new aluminium-lithium alloys are lighter and stiffer; (2) recyclable thermoplastic composites and chemical recycling of epoxy resins could solve the composite end-of-life problem; (3) smelting aluminium and titanium with solar power, as EGA already does for aluminium, lowers the carbon footprint at the source; (4) better corrosion-resistant coatings extend aircraft life and reduce toxic chromate use. A UAE that combines modern fleets, renewable-powered metals and closed-loop recycling can make its aviation sector a model for the region. Materials alone will not achieve net zero, so they must work together with SAF and efficient operations.
